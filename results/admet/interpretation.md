## ADMET Analysis and Interpretation

### Absorption

The selected ligands were checked for how they get absorbed in the intestines. Most of the selected ligands showed good intestinal absorption. Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac and Artemether had low values, which means they can be absorbed easily when taken orally. Metformin and Artemether had higher values but they were still okay.

We also checked how well these ligands can pass through intestinal cell membranes. Almost all of them were able to do this. Metformin and Lumefantrine were a bit weaker at this which might affect how well they can get into the cells in the intestines.

Some of these ligands might be affected by a protein called P-glycoprotein. Metformin had a high chance of being affected by this protein, which means it might not get absorbed as well. On the hand Aspirin, Naproxen, Diclofenac and Artemether were not affected much by this protein.

---

### Distribution

We checked if these ligands can cross the blood-brain barrier. Aspirin, Diclofenac, Caffeine, Theophylline and Lumefantrine can cross it easily. This might be good for drugs that need to affect the brain. It also means they might cause more side effects. Naproxen and Artemether did not cross the barrier easily.

We also checked how much of each ligand gets stuck to proteins in the blood. Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac and Lumefantrine got stuck a lot. When a drug binds strongly to plasma proteins, less free drug is available in circulation. Acetaminophen Caffeine and Theophylline did not get stuck much.

The results also showed how well each ligand can distribute to different tissues in the body. Artemether and Lumefantrine showed broader predicted tissue distribution compared to some of the other ligands. Naproxen did not spread well as the others.

---

### Metabolism

We checked which ligands might be broken down by an enzyme called CYP3A4. Ibuprofen, Acetaminophen, Diclofenac, Caffeine, Theophylline, Metformin and Lumefantrine might be broken down by this enzyme. This means they might interact with drugs that are also broken down by this enzyme.

We also checked if these ligands can stop another enzyme called CYP2D6 from working. Most of them did not affect this enzyme much.. Lumefantrine did affect it a lot, which means it may increase the risk of drug-drug interactions.

The results also showed how stable each ligand is when broken down by the liver. Artemether and Lumefantrine were very stable. Acetaminophen was also relatively stable. Aspirin and Metformin were not very stable which means they might get broken down quickly.

---

### Excretion

We checked how quickly each ligand gets removed from the body. Artemether was removed the fastest, followed by Acetaminophen and Metformin. When a drug is removed quickly it does not stay in the body for long. Naproxen and Diclofenac were removed slowly.

We also checked how long it takes for each ligand to be removed from the body. Most of them were removed within 1-2 hours. Aspirin and Lumefantrine were removed faster within less than 1 hour.

---

### Toxicity

We checked if these ligands might cause heart problems. Most of them did not seem to cause heart problems.. Lumefantrine might cause heart problems because it can affect the heart rhythm.

We also checked if these ligands might cause liver damage. Diclofenac, Ibuprofen, Theophylline, Caffeine, Naproxen and Lumefantrine might cause liver damage. Metformin seemed to be safer for the liver.

Finally we checked if these ligands might cause mutations. Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac and Lumefantrine did not seem to cause mutations. Artemether and Metformin showed higher predicted AMES toxicity values, which may indicate possible mutagenicity concerns, which is a concern.

---

## Overall

Some of the selected ligands, like Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac, Caffeine and Theophylline seemed to be absorbed. Artemether was good at spreading to parts of the body and was stable when broken down.Metformin showed some limitations in predicted absorption and may also have a higher possibility of drug–drug interactions. Lumefantrine had some concerns, with toxicity like causing heart problems and liver damage.

The ADMET analysis helped us understand how these ligands behave in the body how they are broken down and what risks they might pose. This information provides preliminary insight into the pharmacokinetic behavior, metabolism, and toxicity risks of the selected ligands before molecular docking and further computational analysis.
