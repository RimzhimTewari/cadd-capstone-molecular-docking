# Molecular Docking Interpretation

## Objective
The goal of this molecular docking study was to look at how the 7SFB protein structure binds with a set of selected ligand molecules, using PyRx to run the docking. It focused on comparing the binding strength of different compounds and checking how they might interact inside the protein’s predicted binding pocket.

Discovery Studio Visualizer was also used to take a closer look at the protein–ligand interactions, such as hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, and how the docked complexes are positioned.

## Docking Summary
The molecular docking results showed that the selected ligands didn’t all bind to the 7SFB protein structure equally well. The docking scores fell in a range of about -4.7 kcal/mol to -7.0 kcal/mol.

Of the compounds tested, Lumefantrine, Artemether, and Naproxen appeared to bind more strongly, while Metformin had the weakest docking score among the ligands. Overall, most of the ligands showed moderate binding within the predicted binding pocket.


These docking results suggest that several ligands may interact reasonably well with the protein structure, but docking scores on their own aren’t enough to confirm biological activity or real therapeutic potential.


## Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis
### Acetaminophen

Acetaminophen showed a typical hydrogen-bond contact with GLN A:189. It also engaged in pi–alkyl contacts with MET A:49 and HIS A:41, a pattern that points to the ligand being held in place more securely within the binding pocket.

### Artemether

Artemether established a carbon–hydrogen bond with ASP A:187. Alongside that, alkyl contacts involving MET A:49 and MET A:165 appeared, which is consistent with the ligand benefiting from hydrophobic complementarity inside the binding region.

### Aspirin

Aspirin was associated with conventional hydrogen bonding to GLN A:192 and THR A:190. In addition, a pi–alkyl contact with MET A:165 was noted, which could help explain a more stable binding pose.

### Caffeine

Caffeine showed carbon–hydrogen bond interactions with ARG A:188 and MET A:165. A pi–sigma interaction with HIS A:41 was also present, suggesting an extra stabilising contribution within the active site.

### Diclofenac

Diclofenac formed conventional hydrogen bonds with LEU A:141 and HIS A:164. A pi–alkyl interaction with MET A:49 was also detected, supporting the idea that hydrophobic effects play a role in how the ligand sits in the pocket.

### Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen exhibited a conventional hydrogen bond with MET A:49. Beyond that, pi–alkyl and alkyl contacts with MET A:165 and PRO A:168 were observed, implying a binding orientation that is likely helped by hydrophobic packing within the pocket.

### Lumefantrine

Lumefantrine showed a broader set of contacts in the binding pocket, including a carbon–hydrogen bond to ASN A:142 and a pi-donor hydrogen bond with GLU A:166. Several hydrophobic interactions were also seen, involving CYS A:145, LEU A:27, MET A:49, and PRO A:168. A pi–pi T-shaped interaction with HIS A:41 was identified as well, which may add to the overall stabilisation of the docked complex.

### Metformin

Metformin was characterised mainly by Van der Waals contacts with ALA A:191, ARG A:188, and PRO A:168. At the same time, conventional hydrogen bonds with GLN A:192 and THR A:190 were observed within the binding site, indicating that polar contacts still contribute alongside weaker, close-range interactions.

### Naproxen

Naproxen formed a conventional hydrogen bond with MET A:49. It also showed an alkyl contact with PRO A:168 and a pi–alkyl interaction with MET A:165, together with a pi–sigma interaction involving HIS A:41, suggesting a mix of polar anchoring and hydrophobic support in the docking pocket.

### Theophylline

Theophylline demonstrated a conventional hydrogen bond interaction with ARG A:188. A pi–alkyl contact with MET A:165 was also present, which is consistent with the ligand gaining additional stabilisation through hydrophobic interactions within the pocket.
